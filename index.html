The American alligator

An ancient wetland hunter — armored, patient, and essential to the southern marsh.

Explore the species See the range

Species

Built for the blackwater

Alligator mississippiensis has held these waters for millions of years — ambush predator, nest architect, and keystone of the swamp.

Armor

A living fortress

Osteoderms under thick hide, eyes and nostrils set high for a stealth float, and a bite among the strongest in the animal kingdom.

  • Osteoderm armor

    Bony plates beneath the skin shield the back and help regulate heat after a long cool night.

  • Ambush senses

    Pressure receptors along the jaws detect the faintest ripple — dinner announces itself in the dark.

  • Powered by patience

    Long periods of stillness conserve energy until a single explosive strike decides the hunt.

Range

From the Carolinas to the Gulf

Freshwater marshes, cypress swamps, slow rivers, and man-made lakes — anywhere warm water meets soft banks.

Once nearly erased from parts of its range, the American alligator rebounded through legal protection and wetland recovery. It remains a barometer for the health of southern ecosystems.

Protect

Keep the marsh wild

Give alligators space, keep wetlands wet, and never feed a wild gator — a fed alligator is a dead alligator.
